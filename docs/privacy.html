Privacy

What Dota Translator keeps about you, why, for how long, and how to have it deleted. Short, because little is kept.

Last updated: 7 October 2026

Who is responsible

Dota Translator is run by its developer, an individual in the European Union. For anything on this page - a question, a copy of your data, deleting your account - write to support@dotatranslator.live.

Your account

To use the translator you sign in with your e-mail address. There is no password: we send a one-time code and a sign-in link. On our server we keep:

Your e-mail addressTo send your sign-in codes and to know which account is yours. Never sold, never shared, never used for newsletters.
Your PC's IDA one-way fingerprint (a hash) of your Windows machine ID - not the ID itself. It ties the free trial to one PC and an account to one PC at a time, so a trial cannot be had again with a new e-mail.
Your accessWhen your trial started and ends, whether you have paid and until when, and when you last moved your account to another PC.
Sign-in codes and sessionsStored only as one-way fingerprints, never as the code or token itself. Codes expire after 15 minutes; a session after 90 days without use.

Payments

We never see your card. A card payment is made on Swapped.com's own form, which sells USDC (a dollar-pegged coin) and sends it to our wallet on the Polygon network; Swapped handles your card and any identity check under its own privacy policy. If you send USDC yourself, it comes straight from your wallet.

For each order we keep the product, the price, the amount expected, its status, and the transaction it was paid by. A transaction on a public blockchain shows which wallet sent it - that is how blockchains work, and anybody can look it up.

Your chat

  • Only the lines that need translating leave your PC. Lines already in English never do.
  • They go to our translator (translate.dotatranslator.live) and on to Google's Gemini, which does the translating under Google's terms for its paid API. The English comes back the same way.
  • Nothing that is said is written to disk on our server. Recent lines and their translations are held only while the server runs, so a repeated line ("gg", "go mid") is answered at once; they are gone whenever the server restarts.
  • What you send with Ctrl+Enter is kept on your own PC (a file called said.json), so the same words are said the same way next time. You can read or delete it.

What the app does on your PC

It reads the chat from the feed Valve built into the game (Game State Integration), compares two small spots of the screen with hero portraits to show who spoke, and presses keys when you press Ctrl+Enter. None of that is saved or sent anywhere. The app has no analytics and no tracking.

Other things the server sees

  • Your IP address is used while you are connected, to stop abuse (a limit on requests per address per day). It is not stored in our database and we keep no access logs of the translator.
  • Usage counts: how many lines were translated per day and per account, so the daily allowance works and we know what it costs. Counts, not content.
  • Your Steam ID never leaves your PC; the app sends a one-way fingerprint of it at most.

E-mail

Sign-in e-mails are sent through Resend, an e-mail delivery service, which therefore handles your address and the e-mail itself. Mail you send to support@dotatranslator.live is received there as well.

This website

The website (not the app) uses Google Analytics to count visits and clicks - which pages are read, which buttons pressed, roughly which country a visit is from. It sets cookies for that. The site itself is hosted on GitHub Pages and served through Cloudflare, which see your visit as any web host does.

How long it is kept

  • Your account: until you ask us to delete it.
  • Sign-in codes: 15 minutes. Sessions: 90 days from last use.
  • Orders and payments: as long as accounting law requires us to keep records of sales, even after an account is deleted - without anything you did not need to give us.

Your rights

You can ask for a copy of what we keep about you, have it corrected, or have your account deleted - write to support@dotatranslator.live from the address you signed in with. Deleting your account ends your access, and paid time is not refunded by deleting it. You can also complain to your country's data protection authority.

We do not sell your data, do not show ads, and do not use what you say in chat for anything but translating it at that moment.
